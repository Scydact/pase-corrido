# Dominican domino glossary

Compiled 2026-11-06 from web sources (listed at the bottom). No official Dominican rulebook turned up.
Tables differ, so disputed items are marked **disputed** and need confirming with your own table.

## The table

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Frente** | Your partner, seated across from you. |
| **Mano** | The player who puts down the first tile; also the right to open the next hand. In other contexts, one hand of the game. |
| **Manito** | If the mano's opening makes the opponent to the right pass, the mano's partner becomes the manito (the source's wording is ambiguous). |
| **Mirones / el coro** | Onlookers around the table. "Los mirones son de cera": they may not give opinions. |
| **Señas** | Illicit signals between partners. |

## Tiles

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ficha / piedra** | A tile. A double-six set has 28. |
| **Doble** | A tile with the same number on both halves; placed crosswise. |
| **Palo** | All the tiles that share a number (7 per number). |
| **Ficha de autoridad / el burro** | The double six. |
| **La estrella** | The double five. |
| **La caja / la cajita / el muerto / chucha** | The double blank. |
| **Cabeza** | A tile on the table together with the only remaining tile of its number. |

## Play

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lavar / dar agua** | To shuffle. |
| **Salida** | The first play of a hand. |
| **Punta** | One of the two open ends of the line. |
| **Paso** | Passing, only allowed when no tile fits. |
| **Pasar con ficha** | Passing while holding a playable tile; a foul. |
| **Data** | What plays and passes reveal about a hand. |
| **Leer la mesa** | Reading the table from that information. |
| **Repite / mata / tranca** | Strategy terms: repeat a number, kill a play, block the game. |
| **Amarrao** | Playing too carefully. |
| **Dominar** | To play your last tile and win the hand. |

## Special plays and endings

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Capicúa** | Going out with a tile that fits both open ends, which must be different numbers (ends 3 and 5, going out on 3:5). Usually +25, sometimes +30. **Disputed:** most sources exclude doubles; one column says a double counts in some circles. |
| **Tranque** | The game is locked: nobody can play on either end. **Disputed:** see below. |
| **Trancar / virarse** | To make the blocking play; to flip the tiles and count after a tranque. |
| **Tantos** | Points. |
| **Pase de salida** | The opening tile makes the next player pass. Bonus of 10 or 25 where it is played. |
| **Pase corrido / pase redondo** | You play, the other three pass, and you can play again. Usually +25 where it is played. A full block does not count. |
| **Regla General / Regla de Patio** | Count only the opponents' remaining tiles / count all remaining tiles. |

## Usually not Dominican, or inconsistent

| Term | Note |
|---|---|
| **Pollona** | **Disputed:** finishing a game with the other side at zero, recorded as Cuban. One glossary also uses it for capicúa. |
| **Chuchazo** | **Disputed:** a Puerto Rican finish on the double blank (+100 in Quinientos), or more loosely any heavy blow. |
| **Zapato** | **Disputed:** winning a game with the other side at zero (same idea as pollona), or a zero result (recorded as Mexican). |

## Rules confirmed for our table

- **Tranque comparison:** the blocker against the player on their right; the lowest wins.
- **Tranque tie:** goes to the blocker.
- **Capicúa:** a double never counts.

## Where the sources disagree on rules

| Question | Positions found |
|---|---|
| Who compares hands at a tranque | Blocker vs. the player on their right, the lowest wins (FichaFlow, DR1 search excerpt), or combined pair totals (DomiNord, La Mesa). |
| Tranque tie | Goes to the blocker (FichaFlow), the team that started the hand (DomiNord, La Mesa), or nobody scores (general rules). |
| Whose pips score | Only the losers' tiles (Acento, Regla General), or all four hands (FichaFlow default, Regla de Patio). |
| Target score | 100 (Acento), 100 or 200 (DomiNord), 200/250/500 (DR1 search excerpt). |

Agreed by the sources that cover these points: 4 players in 2v2 pairs with partners opposite, 7 tiles each and no
boneyard, play moves to the right (counter-clockwise), the double six opens the first hand, and the winner of a
hand opens the next one.
